## Intent

docs(adr): ADR-0018 — per-attempt review history lives in the event log

Amends ADR-0012 by DECLINING to widen its closed sidecar peer set. The
four permitted peers stand unchanged.

Resolves the SA-1 blocker from the measurement-integrity rev-2 review.
issue-561 and the draft report-rotation.spec.md proposed rotated sibling
files (<spec-stem>.review.<rev>.md) to preserve per-attempt history.
That needs TWO amendments to ADR-0012 — the closed enumeration opened
AND the three-segment naming convention widened to four — to accommodate
one feature, and it carries seven further constraints from review:
the rotation write window (SA-3), a <rev> token colliding with two
existing revision vocabularies (SA-4/CON-3), .blockers.md being
inseparable from .review.md (CON-4), scanner-invisibility as a
load-bearing invariant with its own regression test (CON-5), no
retention bound and an unbounded collision probe (SEC-3), secrets
persisting across attempts (SEC-4), and rotation logic landing in skill
prose rather than lib/cli/artifact.mjs (SA-2).

The observation that dissolves it: an append-only per-attempt store
ALREADY EXISTS. reviewer_report and validator_report events are appended
to .context-index/lifecycle-state/<slug>.jsonl on every run, and
skills/review-specs/SKILL.md already declares that log canonical while
calling .review.md "a presentation/audit artifact for human
consumption". Rotation proposed building a second per-attempt store
beside the existing one, inside the artifact designated as a view.

Decision: keep the peer set closed; record the finding-ID set on
reviewer/validator events (the blocker_id and loop-convergence machinery
already exists); accept that per-attempt finding PROSE is not preserved
— it is the largest, most secret-prone and least machine-useful part,
and the metrics that motivated issue-561 need IDs and verdicts, not
narrative.

Every rotation constraint disappears rather than being solved, because
no new file is created. Per-attempt history also becomes queryable (a
JSONL scan) rather than merely recoverable (git log -p archaeology) —
which is how the 38 hidden BLOCK verdicts had to be found.

Alternatives considered and rejected in the ADR: rotated siblings, a
history directory, git-only status quo, and appending within the single
.review.md.
