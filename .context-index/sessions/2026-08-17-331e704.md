## Intent

docs(review): amend rev 5 — deliver context packs as a path manifest

Rev 4 populated the bundled packs and bounded their size. The bound is
reached on any charter of moderate size, and the pack then delivers a
fraction of what its reviewer's prompt promises.

This is not a contradiction of rev 4 but the completion of its own escape
hatch. Behavior 22m already says the omitted-file list exists "so a reviewer
can Glob/Grep the omitted paths on demand — the existing filesystem-read and
search capabilities make the truncation recoverable rather than silent." Rev 4
therefore already treats paths-plus-reviewer-reads as the recovery path. This
amendment inverts the default: name everything, inline only what must be
byte-exact. Inlining until full and then naming the remainder spends the whole
budget to produce a list the reviewer must act on anyway.

Measured on a632d18f. Omission is TARGET-DEPENDENT — it scales with the target
charter's sibling .spec.md count, because review-base's <charter-dir>/*.spec.md
renders before each pack's distinguishing includes:

  charter (siblings)                    architecture  security     consistency
  review (2)                            complete      complete     complete
  agent-reliable-state-artifacts (12)   27% (9/33)    31% (11/35)  71% (49/69)
  heuristics (14)                       20% (7/35)    24% (9/37)   66% (47/71)

Three defects: declaration-order budget spend drops every ADR from 0009 on;
the security pack loses exactly the two governance files that differentiate it
from architecture (248692 vs 248607 bytes), nullifying rev 4's per-reviewer
split on large charters; and the consistency glob matches 55 cross-cutting
files where only 18 are specs, the other 37 being .review/.plan/.validate/
.blockers sidecars.

Narrowing the glob alone is insufficient — simulated, it takes the 12-sibling
charter from OMITTED 49/69 to OMITTED 13/32 and still reaches the cap. BEH-1
and BEH-3 are therefore both required; neither alone satisfies the contract.

Corrects a premise I initially got wrong. Replacing bodies with paths does NOT
weaken containment: templates/governance/profiles.yaml defines read-only —
which every bundled reviewer profile extends — as allowing filesystem-read and
search with NO path scoping, denying only write, execute and network. Reviewers
can already read any file in the repo, and 22m depends on that. The denylist's
real job is stopping a careless pack author from bulk-inlining secrets via an
over-broad include; BEH-4 preserves it by applying the denylist to the manifest
so denied paths are never named. Reviewer read-scoping is a real pre-existing
gap but belongs to the profile contract, not the pack contract, and is scoped
out.

8 behaviors, 4 added Error Cases rows, 11 acceptance criteria. One criterion
requires verification against a 12+ sibling charter specifically: measuring
against `review` (2 siblings) passes today and proves nothing.

Base spec left immutable (absent from the diff). Full suite 7074/7032/10 —
identical with and without this spec; the 10 failures are tests/repomap ENOENT
on node_modules tree-sitter wasm, this worktree having no node_modules.
