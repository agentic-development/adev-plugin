## Intent

docs(reports): retro on five review rounds, and the two framework gaps behind them

Five /adev:review-specs rounds produced 11 blockers. Only 2 were in the refactor
(155 broken pointers shipped to codex/opencode users, and a path-traversal in the
cursor adapter). The other 9 were in the verification and documentation around it.

One pattern under all of them: intent substituted for evidence. "I wrote a guard"
stood in for the guard failing when the defect is present; "I measured" for the
figure still matching; "I compared the checklists" for the files being equivalent,
in a deletion that lost two sections; "codex and opencode are both mirrors" for
checking opencode, whose row was wrong on both counts.

Four guards could not fail as written. NONE was caught by a green test run — two by
review subagents, two by a hand-run probe. One carried the comment "so this test
cannot silently pass" directly above the branch that was the silent pass.

Two framework gaps amplified a personal failure mode into a systemic one, and are
filed rather than left in the retro:

  adev-plugin-no-falsification-step-973s (P1) — /adev:validate records that gates
  pass; the TDD mandate covers tests that DRIVE behaviour, not tests that GUARD an
  invariant. Nothing in the lifecycle asks whether a new guard detects its defect.
  skill-size-cap.test.mjs already models the right instinct, in exactly one file,
  enforced nowhere.

  adev-plugin-spec-restated-facts-70ff (P2) — the spec template asks the same fact
  to be stated in four or five sections, so every fix has four places to be right.
  This is why "measure more carefully" failed four consecutive revisions: the care
  required scales with the number of restatements, and the restatements are what
  the template asks for.

Report at .context-index/reports/2026-08-19-progressive-disclosure-retro.md, with
what worked recorded alongside what did not — adversarial review found both real
shipped defects, independent lanes converged on the same two findings, and asking a
reviewer for a ruling ended a warning it had restated twice.
