## Intent

docs(context): dissolve measurement-integrity; promote its two hard behaviors

The spec is marked superseded after two review rounds. It grouped seven
fixes by shared motivation ("make measurement trustworthy") rather than
by shared contract — different files, different modules, different
failure modes. Reviewers evaluate contracts, so it fragmented: round 1
blocked on report rotation, round 2 on the check-ID enum, and both were
resolved by removing them rather than revising in place.

What was left touched files another spec's source manifest already
claimed, so it added a competing ownership claim without adding
traceability. Of ten acceptance criteria one was met, and that one was
implemented from its issue before the spec ever passed review — it drove
no implementation.

Its value was as a review trigger, and that value is preserved. The two
review records are retained (32 findings across two rounds); four
changed decisions materially:

  SA-9   an enum sourced only from validate.yaml would reject
         validate.check-1-quality-gates, which skills/validate emits with
         no registry entry by design — deleting Check 1's outcome from
         the event log, and Check 1 is the only check that ever fails
  CON-6  46 distinct validator spellings in the live corpus, ~20
         recoverable by no prefix rule
  CON-1  the frontmatter diagnostic premise was factually wrong
  SEC-1  a command injection live at HEAD (fixed in #214)

Promoted out, each blocked on a real architecture decision:
  check-id-enum.spec.md      (draft, ADR-0010 boundary question)
  report-rotation.spec.md    (draft, ADR-0012 amendment)

Redistributed as direct fixes under the specs that already own their
files: issue-563 (hygiene), issue-564 (sessions), issue-565 (gate
mappings). issue-567, bound 1:1 to the dissolved spec, is closed.

The study gains the generalisable lesson: the review gate earned its
cost on entangled design and was pure overhead on "fix a glob" and
"remove two map entries". The framework applies identical ceremony to
both — the conditional-rigor argument, demonstrated rather than inferred.
